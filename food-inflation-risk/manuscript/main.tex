\begin{abstract}
Food-price monitoring systems usually report conditional means or point
forecasts, even though policy and risk-management decisions are often
driven by the probability of unusually large price increases. This study
defines three-month Food Inflation-at-Risk as the conditional 90th
percentile of forward log food-price inflation and examines how it can
be forecast when the data-generating process changes abruptly and
realised forecast errors arrive with a three-month delay. The empirical
design combines a National Bureau of Statistics development panel
containing 136,826 state-commodity observations for 37 states/FCT and 43
food items, a same-source national continuation through April 2026, and
an external World Food Programme market panel containing 366
market-commodity-unit series. Four leakage-safe expert forecasts are
constructed: a static empirical quantile, a fixed-persistence
exponentially weighted adjustment, a rolling global conformal
calibration, and a partially pooled hierarchical conformal calibration.
A regime-aware meta-controller combines them using 2021 regime priors,
hyperparameters selected in 2022, three-month-delayed expert losses,
softmax weighting, and a partial stress override. On the original 2023
structural-break test, stress-gated recalibration reduced pinball loss
by 38.1\% relative to the static quantile. In the extended backtest, the
meta-controller reduced pooled pinball loss from 5.582 to 4.206 in the
NBS continuation and from 6.679 to 6.111 in the external WFP panel.
Bootstrap comparisons reject equality against the static forecast but
not against the best individual expert. Coverage improved but remained
below the nominal 90\% in the NBS continuation, while the controller
overforecast during the 2025 recovery. The evidence supports
regime-aware forecast aggregation under delayed feedback, but not a
universally optimal adaptive rule. A genuinely untouched country or
future period is required for confirmatory validation.
\end{abstract}
